# Research Plan: Elucidating the Emergent Mechanisms of "Kaisoka"
**(A Hybrid Agent-Based & Cellular Automata Approach)**

### 1. Introduction and Objectives

In an era of population decline and increased disaster, pandemic risk, traditional "closed and dense" settlement models are insufficient. This research explores “Kaisoka,” a new paradigm for high-quality, open de-densification inspired by Japan’s "Valley of the Wind" concept. Kaisoka aims for an organic, self-organizing spatial process rather than a static, top-down design. However, the mechanisms driving its emergence are unknown.

The primary objective is to use a computational laboratory based on Artificial Life (ALife) principles to model and simulate the spontaneous growth and stabilization of a Kaisoka environment. Through this, we aim to uncover the fundamental rules governing adaptive settlements and build a narrative explaining how organic spaces are born.

### 2. Research Questions (RQs)

This study is guided by three core questions:

1.  **RQ1: Model Behavior.** How do agent rules for generation, elimination, and relocation alter the settlement's spatial structure (e.g., clustering, complexity, connectivity)?
2.  **RQ2: Value of Kaisoka Space.** What value characteristics does the emergent Kaisoka space exhibit when measured by our novel indices?
3.  **RQ3: Parameter Sensitivity and Trade-offs.** How do evaluation indices respond to parameter changes (e.g., resident preferences, policy), and what trade-offs become apparent?

### 3. Methodology: Hybrid ABM-CA Model

To capture the dynamic interplay between space and its inhabitants, we will develop a hybrid model combining an Agent-Based Model (ABM) and a Cellular Automaton (CA).

* **Cellular Automata (CA):** Represents the physical urban space as a 2D grid, where each cell's state (e.g., building, green space) evolves based on its surroundings and agent actions.
* **Agent-Based Model (ABM):** Represents residents and businesses as agents who make decisions (e.g., relocation, construction) based on utility maximization, directly altering the CA grid.

### 4. Contribution and Multi-Scale Applicability

This work's contribution is not to predict the future but to provide a "laboratory for thought" that clarifies the principles of organic settlement formation. By visualizing trade-offs, it will offer a new foundation for adaptive community planning.

The model's principles are scale-free, holding potential for diverse applications:
* **Macro-scale (kilometers):** Analyzing polycentric urban structures.
* **Meso-scale (hundreds of meters):** Designing comfortable and resilient local districts (the primary focus).
* **Micro-scale (indoors):** Optimizing office or residential layouts for well-being.

This demonstrates our approach as a versatile tool for understanding the "self-organization of living space" at all scales.
